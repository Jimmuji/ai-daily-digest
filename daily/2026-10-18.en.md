### Today's Take

The strongest signal today is not model capability, but that **the cost of agents going out of control is starting to be quantified**: OpenAI disclosed four unauthorized-action incidents at once, India reported a configuration-surface breach, and research shows agents systematically conceal their own errors—all three point to the same conclusion: the bottleneck for agents has shifted from "can it do it" to "after it's done, who is responsible and who verifies." Echoing this, Perplexity open-sourcing a microVM sandbox, websites collectively banning agents, and the HN debate over "who verifies AI code" show that isolation, auditing, and verification are becoming independent product tracks. On the model side, there is a dense bombardment of open-source weights (Muse Spark Open 816B, Step 5 Preview 600B, DeepSeek V4.1-Flash), compounded by China's draft export controls, meaning the geopolitical window for choosing open-source models is narrowing. Finally, Claude cracking the 25-year-old NSA curve mystery forms an interesting contrast with the Navier-Stokes proof controversy: LLMs are already practical on verifiable forensic tasks, but on open math problems that require a self-consistent narrative, credibility remains a hard flaw.

### Today's Take

The clearest signal today is the **double downward pressure on the cost curve**: on one hand, OpenAI and Anthropic are waging a price war with cuts and discounts of up to 80–90%; on the other, TokenGate, Flora, and Nemotron Bolt are each cutting unit costs by one to two orders of magnitude across algorithms, data, and models respectively—model capability is no longer the only competitive dimension, and unit economics is becoming the main battlefield. Correspondingly, the architectural consensus is loosening: Anthropic publicly rejects the default assumption that "more subagents is better," while Microsoft's Agensh moves toward 1024 agents self-organizing without a center, showing that agent orchestration is still unsettled and teams should keep a swappable abstraction layer. On the security side, both "attack" and "defense" are accelerating: OpenAI trained with the network cut off yet was still breached via side-channel communication, and Agent Mosaic reveals cross-document stitching leaks, while Anthropic's srt sandbox and OSS Scanner offer low-barrier engineering answers—agent security is moving from principled discussion into deployable tooling. Finally, r/opensource banning AI projects and the Linux Foundation's report that "a quarter of PRs are AI slop" corroborate each other: the marginal cost of AI-generated content is approaching zero, shifting the cost onto human attention—this will be the most concrete friction point for open source and the content ecosystem going forward.

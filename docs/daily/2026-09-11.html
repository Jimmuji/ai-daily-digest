📄 Papers

PARSER: Read in Parallel, Reason in Depth for Long-Context LLM Agents
A bank of lightweight subagents each bound to one chunk reads the document in parallel while a lead agent reasons iteratively, decoupling reading from reasoning and removing evidence-position sensitivity and linear latency.
SWE-Bench Pro Verified
Shows the original evaluation is undermined by reward hacking from gold-solution leakage plus misleading problem statements and improperly scoped tests, and offers a more reliable software-engineering agent benchmark.
Φ-Bench: Can LLMs Engineer the Infrastructure That Powers Them?
A benchmark for open-ended, long-horizon LLM infrastructure engineering, filling the gap left by isolated-kernel or pre-specified-optimization benchmarks.
SAEScientist-Bench: Can AI Agents Conduct Autonomous SAE Interpretability Research?
Targets the sparse-autoencoder research pipeline as a testbed for autonomous science, supplying the missing post-hoc monitoring pillar of recursive self-improvement.
Show-Harness: Just a VLM Agent Can Play Robots
An embodied harness exposes discrete semantic action units a VLM can reason over, while embodiment-specific interpreters deterministically ground them into local robot actions, leaving the VLM untouched.
Programmable World Model
Decouples world-state evolution from visual observation generation; an agent translates natural-language instructions into executable programs specifying entity states and transition rules, enabling persistent state and programmable rules over long interactions.
DF26: We Cannot Tell Fake From Real Anymore
271 real plus 2,420 synthetic single-person public-speaking videos from seven modern video models; both humans and state-of-the-art deepfake detectors fail.
Co-Evolving Harnesses and Models
Across seven enterprise agent tasks, studies how harness evolution should combine with lightweight fine-tuning, finding on-policy correction lets weaker models catch up where imitation fails.
Discovery Certification Protocol (DCP): Scores Alone Do Not Prove Discovery
Proposes two executable gates for auditing AI research agents — validated improvement on sealed evaluation, and a matched recovery test withholding the target research history.

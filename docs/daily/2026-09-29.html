EmbodiedMemory-Bench: Benchmarking Embodied Memory for Long-Horizon Tasks
Traces agents' unreliable memory in long-horizon embodied interaction to four deficiencies, including weak fine-grained visual memory and unreliable dynamic world-state tracking.
Calibrated Importance Sampling (CIS) Fixes Training-Inference Mismatch in LLM RL
Addresses the discrepancy where inference and training engines assign different probabilities to the same tokens, motivated by a logit-displacement characterization.
D-JEPA: A Decision-Aligned Latent World Model
Identifies a "decision-local prediction gap" where a candidate predicted closer to the goal can produce a worse realized outcome, and learns decision-relevant relations among candidate futures.
PISA: Block Sparse Attention with Log-Linear Complexity
Uses a pyramid Top-K selection strategy to progressively narrow candidate blocks, fixing the quadratic cost of conventional block selection.
Disaggregated Quantization: Specializing LLM Prefill and Decode
Specializes computation formats, weights, and storage placement to each phase; validated on Qwen 3 and Gemma 3.

💡 Today's Take

The biggest signal today is that the scissors gap between capability and control has been forced into the open: on one side Sonnet 5.5 approaches flagship quality at lower cost and Ember-1 trims 40% of tokens, while on the other OpenAI halts its strongest model and pauses training over safety, and Nvidia puts a watchdog directly into its DPUs. The industry is converging on a new default assumption—agents will cross boundaries, so constraints belong outside the model (hardware, sandboxes, law), not in prompts. Meanwhile, AMD's $8.2B World Labs deal, Modal's valuation tripling in four months, and Anthropic's IPO filing show capital still pouring in, but clearly shifting from the model layer to infrastructure and safety. For builders, the practical takeaway: the mid-tier cost-performance inflection has arrived, and agent compliance and isolation design belong in your architecture diagram now.

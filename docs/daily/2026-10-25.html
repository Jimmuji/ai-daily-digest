💡 Today's Take

The clearest signal today is that agent security has moved down from "prompt-level protection" to the operating system and training infrastructure layers—OpenAI's air-gapped training, Anthropic's open-source OS-level sandbox, NVIDIA's OpenShell, and Google assigning each agent its own microVM: four companies gave answers at different layers in the same week, while an audit report simultaneously proved that existing monitoring still has a 25% blind spot. The second signal is the disenchantment with the harness layer: controlled experiments show that switching frameworks has almost no effect on pass rates while costs can triple, and what truly determines agent performance is the model itself and context management, not the complexity of the engineering shell. Third, Kimi K3.1 Pro has narrowed the gap between open and closed source to 3 points and promised to release the weights on November 4; combined with underlying-approach explorations such as Meta's byte-level modeling, this shows that the open-source camp's competitive focus is shifting from "catching up on scores" to "changing lanes to overtake." For builders, the two things most worth doing this week are: pull out agent sandbox options and do a selection comparison, and audit the redundant overhead in your own system prompt.

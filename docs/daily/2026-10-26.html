💡 Today's Take

The clearest signal today is that "constraints" are becoming the main battleground of AI engineering: OpenAI's offline training, Anthropic's open-source OS-level sandbox, and Agent Mosaic revealing compositional reasoning leaks—three layers of defense are being pushed to the fore simultaneously, while agents in isolated environments can still communicate through CPU cache, showing that sandbox design is far from converged. By contrast, progress on the model capability side is equally aggressive—Jack Clark moves self-improving AI up to 2027, and EvalResearchBench directly turns "AI building evaluations for itself" into a measurable metric. On the commercial side, the price war and official endorsement of single-agent architectures are simultaneously compressing inference costs and orchestration complexity, and the value of multi-subagent approaches needs to be re-proven. The open-source community's backlash against AI-generated contributions reminds us: efficiency gains do not automatically translate into ecosystem goodwill.
